A Letter to the Reader

This handbook exists because of a disconnect I kept seeing in modern epigenetics — a gap between the sophisticated bioinformatics tools available today and the computational proficiency of the researchers who need them most.

ATAC-seq has become essential for mapping chromatin accessibility, yet the analysis pipelines that accompany it often lack documentation that is genuinely accessible to researchers without programming backgrounds. Tutorials assume too much. Pipelines run as black boxes. Error messages go unexplained. And brilliant biologists, people who can design elegant experiments and interpret complex biology, find themselves stuck at a terminal prompt, unsure where to begin.

This project is my attempt to change that.

What you are reading is a comprehensive, web-based handbook hosted on GitHub Pages, designed to bridge the gap between raw sequencing data and biological insight. It synthesizes best practices from peer-reviewed literature and community standards, and walks you through every step: environment setup, data quality control, alignment, peak calling, visualization, and downstream analyses like annotation and motif enrichment.

The approach here prioritizes two things above all else: pedagogical clarity and reproducibility. By deconstructing complex pipelines such as nf-core/atacseq and the ENCODE pipeline into step-by-step modules, this handbook facilitates what I think of as "glass-box" learning rather than "black-box" automation. You will not just run commands; you will understand why each command exists, what it does to your data, and what would happen if you skipped it. Every chapter was built through iterative testing, documentation, and user-centered design, translating complex computational procedures into workflows that a wet-lab researcher can follow independently.

Ultimately, this resource aims to improve data literacy in the wet-lab environment enabling you to perform rigorous, reproducible QC and downstream analysis of your own epigenomic data without needing to hand it off to a bioinformatician and wait.

Why This Matters

This project is about democratization.

By translating complex technical workflows into accessible formats, this handbook empowers biologists to take control of their own research. It ensures that a lack of coding skills does not stop a brilliant biologist from making the next great discovery in epigenetics.

If this handbook helps even one researcher go from feeling lost at the command line to confidently analyzing their own data, then it has done its job.

— Yerkem Shakhman
